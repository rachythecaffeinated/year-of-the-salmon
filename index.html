Goals

Put your name in the form above, then tick these off as you hit them. The dates are where each one is expected to fall, not a deadline — tick it whenever it actually happens. The tracks overlap on purpose: you'll be doing hang work and row work long after the pull-up work starts.

Reality check

The pull-up is realistic. The ladder is genuinely hard. Going from a near-zero start to a first strict pull-up in about seven months is a normal outcome if you train it three times a week and don't skip the boring row work. Plenty of people get there faster.

The salmon ladder is a different animal. It needs explosive power and timing, not just strength — there are people who can do ten strict pull-ups and still can't move a rung. Five rungs inside a year from scratch is an ambitious goal, not a foregone conclusion, and it is the reason the plan stops chasing pull-up rep counts at five and spends the back half of the year on the skill itself.

If you're here for the walk and the lifting, that counts. Most people in this challenge aren't chasing the ladder at all. The step target and the Base track stand on their own — log those, ignore the rest, and you're still in the run.

Milestone dates assume roughly three sessions a week. Missing a week doesn't put you behind; it moves everything right by a week. Tendons adapt slower than muscle, so the hang progression is deliberately unhurried — pushing it is how people end up with elbow tendinopathy in month three and lose the year.
